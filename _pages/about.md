#### Hi, I'm Julie!

I'm a PhD candidate in the Cybernetics Group at Utrecht University's Department of Information and Computing Sciences, with an expected graduation in November 2026.

**What I Work On**

My research focuses on **handling non-standard cases in computing**—both from users' and models' perspectives. In practice, this means I work at the intersection of **accessibility** (ensuring users with impairments aren't left behind by technology) and **machine learning robustness** (tackling data imbalance and generalizability challenges). A significant part of my work involves working with **biosignals for affect recognition**, which sits nicely at the crossroads of my broader interests in affective computing, human-machine interaction, machine learning, and social robotics.

**How I Got Here**

I graduated **valedictorian** from UPSSITECH in Toulouse, France, where I studied Robotics and Interactive Systems. During my studies, I completed two research internships abroad: one on interactive reinforcement learning for robotic tasks (Aarhus University, Denmark), and another in social robotics (University of Sherbrooke and University of Waterloo, Canada). Those experiences sparked something in me; I realized I wanted to keep asking questions, looking for answers, and building things that matter. So here I am, in academia.

**What Drives Me**

At the core of everything I do is a simple goal: **conduct research with tangible social impact**. I'm drawn to problems where better technology can genuinely improve people's lives, particularly for those who are often overlooked in mainstream tech development.

**Outside the Lab**

When I'm not thinking about accessibility, biosignals, or biased datasets, you'll find me with a book, out for a run, crocheting something cozy, or spending time with my dog.
